Most shops are built by a lot of people. One person does the brand. Another designs the store. Someone else builds it, and someone else again wires up the orders. Every handover loses a little. A font gets swapped, an animation gets cut, a button stops feeling right.

I did all of it myself, so nothing got lost in between. WhyClub is a streetwear label, and I made every part of it: how it looks, how the store feels in your hand, and the system that takes an order from "paid" to "it's home".

## A store that feels like the brand

The brand is loud, so the store gets out of its way. Each drop gets a campaign image that fills the screen. Shopping happens on quiet neutrals, so the clothes do the talking.

<Screens device="phone" caption="The screens from my home page. Tap through them, or switch to desktop." />

The shop is a plain grid of tees with prices. No badges fighting for attention, no carousel inside a carousel. You see the clothes, you see what they cost, you pick one.

## Phone first

Clothes get bought on phones, so the phone came first and the desktop store grew out of it.

Navigation lives in a dock at the bottom of the screen, where your thumb already is. Tap **Shop** and the dock morphs into a sheet with Women, Men, Sale and your collection.

<Video src="/assets/work/whyclub/p-nav.mp4" webm="/assets/work/whyclub/p-nav.webm" poster="/assets/work/whyclub/p-nav.webp" alt="Tapping Shop in the mobile dock morphs it into a sheet with Women, Men, Sale and My collection." caption="Shop opens out of the dock instead of jumping to a new page." phone />

Adding to the bag shouldn't need a whole page. Quick add on a product card opens a small size sheet. Pick a size and it's in.

<Video src="/assets/work/whyclub/p-quickadd.mp4" webm="/assets/work/whyclub/p-quickadd.webm" poster="/assets/work/whyclub/p-quickadd.webp" alt="Tapping quick add on a product card opens a size sheet; choosing a size adds it to the bag." caption="Quick add: card, size, done." phone />

On the product page, the details rise over the photo as a sheet, so the tee never leaves the screen. Details, Size & fit and Delivery sit in that one sheet as tabs.

## After you order

Ordering is usually where a store goes quiet: a confirmation email and a tracking link. I wanted it to be the best part.

Every order gets a pass. It prints in, then changes as the order moves: *You're in*, *All packed*, *On its way*, *It's home*. Each stage has its own shader, written by hand in WebGL, so the pass is a small piece of live light rather than a status label.

<Video src="/assets/work/whyclub/p-pass.mp4" webm="/assets/work/whyclub/p-pass.webm" poster="/assets/work/whyclub/p-pass.webp" alt="The Drop order pass prints in, then moves through confirmed, packed, shipped and delivered, each with its own shader." caption="The order pass, from confirmed to delivered." phone />

The same hand-written shaders turn up around the store: riso-style badges for the beta pass, a dithered asterisk, and a 404 page where a prism splits the light.

<Figure src={notFound} alt="The WhyClub 404 page: a prism splitting light through a 4, a triangle and a 4." caption="The 404, made of light." wide />

You can [try the drop pass](https://prototype.whyclub.in/) yourself, or read [how the shaders were built](https://prototype.whyclub.in/case-study/).

## Behind the counter

The part you don't see is the part that runs every order. I built the backend too: it takes the order, keeps the pass in step with the parcel, and gives me the tools I need to put out each release.

Doing both halves means the details survive. The sheet that rises over the photo, the dock that opens into a menu, the pass that changes when your parcel ships: none of it had to be explained to anyone. I just built it.
